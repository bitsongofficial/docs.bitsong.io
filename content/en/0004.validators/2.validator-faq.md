# Validator FAQ

Find answers to common questions about the role of Validators in the BitSong network, from basic concepts and incentives to technical requirements and security practices.

::note{to="/en/delegators"}
If you are looking for information on how to stake your tokens without running a node, please visit the **Delegators** section.
::

## General Concepts

::accordion

:::accordion-item{label="What is a Validator?" icon="i-lucide-server"}
A **Validator** is an entity that operates a full node and validates transactions to secure the BitSong blockchain. In return for their services, validators earn rewards.

Validators participate in:
- **Consensus:** Voting on the validity of blocks.
- **Governance:** Voting on network proposals.

Users declare their intention to become a validator by sending a `create-validator` transaction to become a candidate. The total amount of staked tokens determines the validator's voting power and probability of being selected to produce blocks.

Currently, only the **top 64 Validators** by staked BTSG participate in consensus.
:::

:::accordion-item{label="What is Staking?" icon="i-lucide-lock"}
Staking is the act of depositing cryptocurrencies to help secure the BitSong blockchain.

- **Delegators** stake their BTSG tokens to a Validator for a share of the rewards.
- **Validators** stake BTSG tokens (their own + delegators') to participate in the network.
:::

:::accordion-item{label="What is a Full Node?" icon="i-lucide-monitor"}
A **full node** runs the current version of the BitSong software to validate transactions and blocks. Operating a full node requires running non-compromised, up-to-date software with low network latency and zero downtime.

BitSong welcomes users to run full nodes even if they do not plan to become Validators.
:::

:::accordion-item{label="What is a Delegator?" icon="i-lucide-users"}
A **Delegator** is a token holder who delegates their BTSG to a Validator to help secure the network in return for rewards. Unlike Validators, Delegators do not need specific hardware.

**Responsibilities:**
- Actively monitor their stake and Validator performance.
- Participate in governance votes.
- Manage risk (e.g., by staking to multiple Validators).

::warning
If a Validator misbehaves, their stake (including delegators' funds) may be slashed. Always do your own research.
::
:::

::

## Becoming a Validator

::accordion

:::accordion-item{label="How do I become a validator?" icon="i-lucide-rocket"}
To become a Validator, you must send a `create-validator` transaction with the following parameters:

- **Validator's `PubKey`:** Private key used to sign _prevotes_ and _precommits_.
- **Validator's Address:** Application-level address for public identification, delegation, and claiming rewards.
- **Moniker:** The validator's name.
- **Website:** (Optional) Link to your website.
- **Description:** (Optional) Short bio.
- **Initial commission rate:** Fees charged to delegators on rewards.
- **Maximum commission:** The absolute cap on your commission rate (cannot be changed later).
- **Commission max change rate:** Maximum daily increase allowed for commission.
- **Minimum self-delegation:** Minimum BTSG you must keep bonded. If you fall below this, you unbond.

Once created, you become a candidate. You must be in the **top 64** by total stake to join the active set and earn rewards.
:::

:::accordion-item{label="How can I join the testnet?" icon="i-lucide-test-tube-2"}
We strongly recommend testing your setup on the Testnet before launching on Mainnet. This signals readiness to the community.

::card-group
::card{title="Join the Testnet" icon="i-lucide-external-link" to="https://hub.cosmos.network/main/gaia-tutorials/join-testnet.html" target="_blank"}
Follow the tutorial to join the test network.
::
::card{title="Testnet Repository" icon="i-simple-icons-github" to="https://github.com/cosmos/testnets" target="_blank"}
View the official repository for testnet details.
::

:::accordion-item{label="What are the different types of keys?" icon="i-lucide-key"}
Validators manage two primary types of keys:

1.  **Tendermint Key**
    - Unique key used to sign consensus votes.
    - Associated with `cosmosvalconspub`.
    - Generated during node creation (`gaiad init`).

2.  **Application Key**
    - Used to sign transactions.
    - Associated with `cosmospub` (public) and `cosmos` (address).
    - Derived from `gaiad keys add`.

::note
A validator's operator key uses reserved prefixes `cosmosvaloper` and `cosmosvaloperpub`.
::
:::

:::accordion-item{label="What states can a validator be in?" icon="i-lucide-activity"}
After sending `create-validator`, a validator exists in one of three states:

- :badge[in validator set]{color="success"} **Active:** In the top 64. Participating in consensus, earning rewards, subject to slashing.
- :badge[jailed]{color="error"} **Jailed:** Misbehaved (downtime or double-signing). Not participating or earning.
    - **Offline:** Can `unjail` to return.
    - **Double-signing:** Cannot unjail.
- :badge[unbonded]{color="neutral"} **Unbonded:** Not in the active set. Not signing or earning. Cannot be slashed.
:::

:::accordion-item{label="What is self-delegation?" icon="i-lucide-coins"}
**Self-delegation** is the amount of BTSG a Validator bonds from their own wallet to themselves. It demonstrates "skin in the game."

- **Minimum:** `1 ubtsg` (micro-unit) or effectively `1 BTSG` depending on network config.
- **Increasing:** Send a `delegate` transaction from your application key.
:::

:::accordion-item{label="How do delegators choose their validators?" icon="i-lucide-search-check"}
Delegators typically consider:

- **Self-delegation:** Higher amounts imply more trust and risk-sharing.
- **Total Stake:** Indicates voting power. Larger validators produce more blocks but dilute rewards among more delegators.
- **Commission Rate:** Lower commission means higher rewards for delegators, but validators need revenue to sustain operations.
- **Track Record:** Seniority, uptime history, and governance participation.
- **Reputation:** Audits, community contributions, and transparency.

::tip
Check out this [guide on due diligence](https://medium.com/@interchain_io/3d0faf10ce6f) for more insights.
::
:::

## Responsibilities & Governance

::accordion

:::accordion-item{label="Do Validators need to be publicly identified?" icon="i-lucide-fingerprint"}
**No.** There is no requirement for individuals or entities to reveal their identity. However, validators can choose to link a website or description to build trust with delegators.
:::

:::accordion-item{label="What are the main responsibilities?" icon="i-lucide-list-checks"}
1.  **Consensus:** Run correct software, ensure high uptime, and secure private keys.
2.  **Governance:** Vote on proposals.
3.  **Community:** Participate in discussions and stay updated on ecosystem events.
:::

:::accordion-item{label="What does governance participation involve?" icon="i-lucide-vote"}
Validators **must** vote on proposals (e.g., parameter changes, upgrades).

::note
Validators represent the vote of their **absent Delegators**. If a delegator does not vote personally, they inherit their validator's vote.
::
:::

:::accordion-item{label="What does staking imply?" icon="i-lucide-lock"}
Staking is a safety deposit.

- **Unbonding:** Requires a **3-week unbonding period**.
- **Slashing Risk:** During unbonding, tokens are still liable for slashing if misbehavior occurred *before* the unbonding started.
- **Custody:** Validators **never** have custody of delegator funds. They cannot steal tokens.
:::

:::accordion-item{label="How is the block proposer chosen?" icon="i-lucide-dices"}
Proposers are selected **deterministically** based on bonded stake.
- If a validator holds **10%** of total bonded stake, they will be selected as proposer **10%** of the time.
:::

::

## Incentives & Rewards

::accordion

:::accordion-item{label="What are the incentives?" icon="i-lucide-gift"}
**For Stakers:**
- **Block Rewards:** Inflationary BTSG minted per block.
- **Transaction Fees:** Fees paid for network usage.

**For Validators:**
- **Commission:** A percentage cut of the rewards earned by their delegators.
- **Governance Power:** Influence over the network's future.
:::

:::accordion-item{label="How are block rewards distributed?" icon="i-lucide-calculator"}
Rewards are spread among validators and then to delegators.

**Example:**
- Block Reward: `1000 BTSG`
- Validator Pool: 10 Validators (equal stake), each with 20% self-bond.
- Commission: 1%

**Calculation for one pool (100 BTSG allocation):**
1.  **Commission:** `100 * 80% (delegators) * 1% = 0.8 BTSG`
2.  **Validator Gets:** `100 * 20% (self) + 0.8 = 20.8 BTSG`
3.  **Delegators Get:** `100 * 80% - 0.8 = 79.2 BTSG` (distributed proportionally)
:::

:::accordion-item{label="How are fees distributed?" icon="i-lucide-receipt"}
Fees follow a similar model but include a **Proposer Bonus** (1% to 5%) for including precommits.

**Example:**
- Total Fees: `1025.51 BTSG`
- **Community Pool Tax (2%):** `20.51 BTSG` deducted.
- Remaining: `1005 BTSG`

**If Proposer includes 100% signatures (5% bonus):**
- **Proposer Pool:** Receives `105 BTSG` (Base + Bonus).
- **Other Pools:** Receive `100 BTSG`.

Rewards are then split between Validator and Delegators based on commission and stake share.
:::

:::accordion-item{label="What are the slashing conditions?" icon="i-lucide-triangle-alert"}
Validators can lose stake for misbehavior:

::warning
**Double Signing**
Signing two blocks at the same height.
**Penalty:** 5% stake slashed. Cannot unjail.
::

::warning
**Downtime**
Offline for >95% of the last 10,000 blocks.
**Penalty:** 0.01% stake slashed. Can unjail.
::
:::

:::accordion-item{label="How is decentralization preserved?" icon="i-lucide-network"}
- **Penalty-free re-delegation:** Delegators can easily switch validators.
- **UI Warnings:** Wallets may warn users when delegating to validators who already hold too much power.
:::

## Technical Requirements

::accordion

:::accordion-item{label="Hardware & Software" icon="i-lucide-cpu"}
**Hardware:**
- Data center location with redundant power/network.
- Dedicated servers (specs rise with network usage).
- Firewall and HSM (Hardware Security Module).

**Software:**
- BitSong full node.
- Monitoring and alerting solutions.
:::

:::accordion-item{label="Key Management (HSM)" icon="i-lucide-shield-check"}
Validators should use an HSM supporting `ed25519` keys. Options include:
- YubiHSM 2
- Ledger Nano S
- Ledger BOLOS SGX enclave
- Thales nShield
:::

:::accordion-item{label="DDoS Protection & Sentry Nodes" icon="i-lucide-shield"}
Validators mitigate Denial-of-Service (DoS) attacks using **Sentry Node Architecture**.

1.  **Validator Node:** Runs in a private subnet. Connects *only* to trusted Sentry Nodes.
2.  **Sentry Nodes:** Public-facing full nodes. They handle the internet traffic and relay blocks to the validator.

If a sentry node is attacked, the validator remains safe and can spin up new sentries.

::card{title="Sentry Node Architecture" icon="i-lucide-external-link" to="https://forum.cosmos.network/t/sentry-node-architecture-overview/454" target="_blank"}
Read the detailed overview on the Cosmos Forum.
::
:::

:::accordion-item{label="Operational Logistics" icon="i-lucide-briefcase"}
Running a validator requires:
- **Skilled Team:** Often multiple people for 24/7 coverage.
- **Maintenance:** Regular upgrades and bug fixes.
- **Incident Response:** Ability to respond immediately to outages or attacks.
:::
